STRUCTURE: 4 TOWERS x 81 SEATS, GROUNDED ON 0
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A. TOWER STATE  (corrected — the original was incoherent)
   ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
   The original read "each tower holds state in {-1, 0, +1}" while also giving
   the towers moduli 16, 27, 5 and 463. A tower cannot be one trit and mod 463
   at the same time. What is true:

     each tower holds a residue          t_i in [0, m_i)
     each tower is WRITTEN in balanced ternary, w_i trits wide
     a trit is the digit; the tower is a multi-trit word

   Tower 1: modulus 16
   Tower 2: modulus 27
   Tower 3: modulus 5
   Tower 4: modulus 463
   -----------------------
   Combined: 16 x 27 x 5 x 463 = 1,000,080 unique seats     [verified]

   Pairwise coprime: yes. 463 is prime.                     [verified]

B. THE TWO ENCODINGS  (this replaces rev 1's overcall)
   ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
   There are two legitimate ways to address a seat, and every figure in this
   document must state which one it is using.

   TOWER-SEPARATE — each tower written as its own word, towers stay
   independently addressable:

     Tower 1 (mod  16)   4 bits   3 trits   3^3 =  27   slack  11
     Tower 2 (mod  27)   5 bits   3 trits   3^3 =  27   slack   0   EXACT
     Tower 3 (mod   5)   3 bits   2 trits   3^2 =   9   slack   4
     Tower 4 (mod 463)   9 bits   6 trits   3^6 = 729   slack 266
                        -------  --------
     per seat            21 bits  14 trits
     for 81 seats     1,701 bits  1,134 trits

   JOINT — the whole address flattened to one number, tower boundaries gone:

     2^19 =   524,288  too small        3^12 =   531,441  too small
     2^20 = 1,048,576  fits             3^13 = 1,594,323  fits

     per seat            20 bits  13 trits
     for 81 seats     1,620 bits  1,053 trits

   *** 21 pairs with 14. 20 pairs with 13. Do not cross them. ***

   The original document chose tower-separate for trits (14) and joint for
   bits (20). Both figures are individually right. Putting them in one
   comparison is what produced the phantom overhead in section G.

   Tower-separate costs 1 bit and 1 trit more per seat. That is what
   independent tower addressing costs. It is a design choice, not waste.

C. INTEGER ENCODING  — CORRECTED
   ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Mixed-radix positional decomposition (NOT CRT — see note):

     address = t1*62505 + t2*2315 + t3*463 + t4

     place values:  27*5*463 = 62,505
                       5*463 =  2,315
                         463 =    463
                                    1

   *** The original used 6345 for the t1 place. Maximum reachable with 6345 is
   157,679 out of 1,000,079 — the map cannot cover the space and therefore
   collides. With 62,505 it is a bijection: exhaustively generated all
   1,000,080 addresses, got 1,000,080 distinct, 0 collisions, full coverage. ***

   NOTE ON THE LABEL: the formula above is mixed-radix positional, not the
   Chinese Remainder Theorem. CRT does genuinely apply here — the moduli are
   pairwise coprime — but CRT is a different map (reconstruct from residues via
   modular inverses). Both are valid bijections onto the same space. They are
   not the same function and should not share a name.

D. FLOAT REPRESENTATION
   ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Per seat:   4 x 64 = 256 bits
   Per 81:     20,736 bits
   Overhead vs joint integer: 20,736 - 1,620 = 19,116 bits

   This section is why shared keys exist: float cannot be trusted to carry
   identity. IEEE-754 has two zeros (+0.0 and -0.0) that compare equal but do
   not hash equal, so a == b and hash(a) == hash(b) come apart. Int and trit
   each have exactly one zero, so for them equality and byte-identity are the
   same relation.

G. STORAGE — CORRECTED
   ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
   *** Defect 1: the original held two statements that cannot both be true — a
   table row saying ternary costs +10.0% overhead, and a line below saying
   ternary is 1.538x denser. ***

   *** Defect 2, and this is the one that generated defect 1: the row compared
   14 trits (TOWER-SEPARATE) against 20 bits (JOINT). Mixed encodings. ***

   Compared like with like:

     encoding          bits    trits
     ---------------------------------
     tower-separate      21       14
     joint               20       13

   What 1.538 actually is: 1,620 bits / 1,053 trits. That is a ratio of SYMBOL
   COUNTS in two different alphabets. It is not a compression ratio. A trit is
   not free — it costs log2(3) to represent — so fewer symbols does not mean
   fewer resources.

   Slack, in exact integers, joint encoding, no logarithms:

     encoding     per seat   capacity      holds        slack
     ---------------------------------------------------------------
     binary       20 bits    1,048,576     1,000,080     48,496
     ternary      13 trits   1,594,323     1,000,080    594,243

     ternary slack / binary slack = 12.2534
     (the audit receipt prints 12x — integer division truncating 12.2534)

   For THIS space size, binary is the tighter fit. 1,000,080 sits just above
   2^19 and far below 3^13, so the ternary ceiling overshoots.

   Where ternary genuinely wins is radix economy — b/ln(b) is minimised at
   b = 3, beating 2 and 4 — but that is a statement about cost per digit in
   hardware, not about how tightly this particular address space packs.

   Neither encoding is "exact" here. 1,000,080 is not a power of 2 and not a
   power of 3, so both waste states. Only Tower 2 (mod 27 = 3^3) is exact, and
   it is exact in trits and not in bits.
